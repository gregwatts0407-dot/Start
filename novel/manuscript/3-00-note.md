# PART THREE {.part}

::: {.parttitle}
The Grey Room

*by Hester Quaile*
:::

## Editor's Note {.note}

Hester Mary Quaile wrote this in the summer of 1995, in three red exercise books, at a hospital in York where she was then a patient. A doctor there, a Dr Imrie, had asked her to set down what had happened to her at Compline, in order, without stopping to decide whether she believed it. She told me that it was the best advice she was ever given and that she has passed it on to a great many people since.

She did not write it for print. She became a writer afterwards; under her initials she has published five novels, every one of which has a house in it, and she is alive and well and lives on the north-east coast with a view of the sea that she says she chose on purpose. When I asked whether I might use the exercise books she took a week to answer. Then she said yes, on one condition, which was that I should not correct them.

I have not corrected them. That includes the places where she and I both now know that there is another explanation for what she saw. You will find that she knows it too, in the writing itself. She was a trained nurse and a careful person, and I do not think there is a single page on which she fails to tell you what a sensible woman would conclude. She tells you, and then she tells you what she saw.

I am not going to say which of those you should prefer. I will say that I have stood in the long gallery at Compline at midnight, in October, and that I would not have stayed to take notes.

Two practical matters. Miss Quaile arrived at Compline three weeks before the night in question, and so she alone of the six gives you the house as it was beforehand. I have let that stand at its full length, since without it you will not understand what the others walked into. And her times are house times, read from the clocks round her, of which there was always one in sight. She had been told on her first day to put her own watch back an hour. She did so, and never changed it again while she lived there.

You will meet in these pages a conversation you have read once already, in Mr Pargeter's account. I have compared the two versions word for word. They agree.
