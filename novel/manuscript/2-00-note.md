# PART TWO {.part}

::: {.parttitle}
The Compline Business

*from* Mostly My Fault *by Toby Pargeter*
:::

## Editor's Note {.note}

Tobias Pargeter is a wine merchant, and at the time of writing is alive, solvent, and married for the third time, in that order of likelihood by his own account. In 2003 he had three hundred copies printed, at his own expense, of a volume of reminiscences called *Mostly My Fault*, which he distributed, the flyleaf says, "to friends, creditors, and wives, where these could be told apart." What follows is the eleventh chapter of that book, complete. He broke it into seven sections and I have kept his divisions and his headings.

It began life as an after-dinner story. I have spoken to several people who heard him tell it in the nineteen-nineties, at wine-trade suppers and at two weddings, and they agree that it was the best thing he did and that it ran about twenty-five minutes, and that he always stopped at the same place. The printed chapter does not stop there. It goes on for some two pages more, and those two pages were never part of the performance. His wife, the current one, told me that he wrote them in a single evening and would not have the book printed without them.

You will observe that Mr Pargeter is a comic writer and that he knows it. I ask you not to hold that against his evidence. A man who is trying to make you laugh has a strong motive to describe exactly what people did, because that is where the laughs are. He is, on matters of fact, the most scrupulous witness of the six. I have checked him against the dimensions of the stable yard, the weight of the object in question, and the recollections of Gail Pask, who is now Mrs Gail Thurlow and keeps a garden centre near Tiptree, and I have not caught him out once.

His watch was in the Font from seven o'clock. Every time he gives is house time, and most are from the clock on the kitchen wall. That clock was kept eight minutes fast. Mrs Pask set it so herself, every Monday, on the principle that a dinner which thinks it is late will come up on time, and she told nobody. Mr Pargeter did not know it. Nor, when he looked at the same clock an hour later, did Mr Cade.

You have just read that a man was dead. You are about to read that he was not. I remind you of what I said at the start, and I ask you to put a finger on the place.
